## 2. Why this proposal is different: it already works

Most proposals describe what will be built. Chakshi is **already in production today.** The working platform demonstrates real engineering execution, not slideware:

- **Live eCourts case intelligence** — automated, compliant search of the national eCourts system by **CNR number** and by **party name**, plus retrieval of **case status, orders/judgments, cause lists and caveats**.
- **Compliance by design** — the system **never bypasses CAPTCHA**. A human solves every CAPTCHA; the software only handles navigation, form-filling and structured reading of **publicly available** court data. This respects eCourts' terms of service and keeps the platform on the right side of the law from day one.
- **Structured extraction** — pleadings and case records are parsed into clean, structured data (parties, dates, status, orders, interlocutory applications, business/proceedings).
- **Engineered for resilience** — the scrapers already survive eCourts' redesigned markup, with hardened selectors and graceful failure handling — evidence of a maintained, production-grade codebase, not a demo.

## 3. The problem we are solving for Tamil Nadu

District-level and city-level legal practice in Tamil Nadu still runs on scattered case files — PDFs, physical bundles, WhatsApp, email, handwritten notes and old office records. Before a single draft is written, advocates, junior advocates and clerks spend **hours** manually reading, classifying, summarising and cross-checking bundles.

This costs the profession time, costs clients money, and — because manual review is fallible — occasionally costs cases (missed limitation, missed contradictions, missed documents).

Tamil Nadu has **more than one lakh enrolled advocates** and a district judiciary carrying a pendency in the **lakhs of cases** (per the National Judicial Data Grid). The efficiency dividend from giving every one of those advocates a reliable digital case-preparation assistant is enormous — and it compounds across hearings, courts and years.

Chakshi reduces that friction **while keeping the advocate as the final professional decision-maker.** It is legal *productivity and intelligence software* — never a substitute for the advocate's judgement.

## 4. What Chakshi is — a matter-first legal operating system

Chakshi is **not** a generic chatbot. The central unit is the **Matter / Case / Client File.** Every workflow flows from the matter:

**Live today (eCourts intelligence layer):** case discovery by CNR/party, case status, orders & judgments, cause lists, caveats, structured case-detail extraction.

**Built over the 18-month plan (matter workspace):**
document upload → OCR & case-file organisation → AI reading of pleadings/notices/orders/agreements/evidence/judgments → fact extraction → party matrix → chronology → evidence mapping → legal-issue identification → contradiction detection → missing-document list → limitation warnings → matter-specific legal research → drafting *from the case record* → hearing tracking → clerk/junior task assignment → client notes → billing references → court-ready outputs.

**The flagship workflow:** *an advocate uploads a full case bundle, and Chakshi returns a usable case brief, timeline, issue list, evidence chart, contradiction list, missing-document list, limitation warning, draft strategy note and first draft* — each item traceable back to the source page.

## 5. Responsible AI: "Verify or classify" — never confident-but-wrong

Legal AI cannot be allowed to hallucinate. Chakshi's core safety principle is **Verify or classify.** Every AI output must show:

1. **Source document** and **page reference** (where available)
2. The **extracted quote** that supports the answer
3. A **confidence level**
4. A **missing-information warning** where the record is incomplete
5. An explicit **"Not found in record"** response when the record does not support an answer
6. A clear **lawyer-review boundary** on every professional output

This "show your work" design is the difference between a tool a court-facing professional can trust and one they cannot. It is also exactly the kind of **auditable, explainable AI** a government should want to be associated with in a sensitive domain.

## 6. Trust, compliance and guardrails (the part government due-diligence cares about)

We have engineered Chakshi to clear the bars that usually sink legal-tech and govtech projects:

- **Data sovereignty & residency.** All matter data can be hosted **within India / Tamil Nadu** on the Department's preferred infrastructure. No advocate or client data leaves the jurisdiction.
- **DPDP Act, 2023 compliance.** Consent, purpose limitation, data-minimisation, retention controls, audit logs and a documented data-protection posture are built into the architecture (₹25 lakh of the budget is ring-fenced for exactly this).
- **Bar Council ethics — designed in.** Chakshi is **software**, not a law firm, advocate-replacement, legal-advice marketplace or referral engine. It does **not** solicit clients, does **not** offer legal advice, and does **not** rank or recommend advocates. This keeps it fully consistent with Bar Council of India / Tamil Nadu rules on advertising and professional conduct — a critical distinction, given the Founder is himself a practising advocate.
- **Lawful data access.** Only publicly available court data, accessed in a terms-respecting, human-CAPTCHA manner — no scraping of protected systems, no automation of authentication.
- **Human-in-the-loop by default.** The advocate signs off on every output. Chakshi accelerates judgement; it never replaces it.
